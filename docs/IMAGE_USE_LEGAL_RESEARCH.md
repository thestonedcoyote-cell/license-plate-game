# Plate Image Use — Legal Research Notes

**Status:** working product research, not legal advice or final clearance.

## 1. State-published does not automatically mean public domain

17 U.S.C. §105 applies to works of the United States federal government, not automatically to state/local government publications. State DMV webpages and brochures can therefore contain copyrighted material, including third-party sponsor artwork.

## 2. Facts and specifications are different from expression

Copyright does not protect facts, ideas, systems, methods, or procedures. The product strategy therefore includes independent reconstruction from statutes, regulations, manufacturing/technical specifications, and factual descriptions. Production images can be used as verification references without treating their pixels as the source of the public asset.

Government edicts such as legislation, regulations, administrative rulings, and comparable official legal materials are also treated differently from ordinary agency artwork/publications under the government-edicts doctrine.

## 3. Redrawing is not a magic eraser

If the visual identity of a specialty plate depends on original illustration, a mascot, logo, sponsor art, or other creative expression, merely tracing/redrawing it does not by itself eliminate copyright or trademark issues. Such elements receive separate provenance/rights review.

## 4. Small identification thumbnails are a separate fair-use theory worth evaluating

In *Perfect 10, Inc. v. Amazon.com, Inc.*, 508 F.3d 1146 (9th Cir. 2007), the Ninth Circuit held Google's stored thumbnail copies were fair use after a case-specific four-factor analysis, emphasizing the thumbnails' different function, public benefit, and lack of substitution for full-size images. The court also noted that copying an entire image can still be fair where the whole image is reasonably necessary for the new function.

License Plate Game has a different factual context, so this is **not automatic clearance**. But it is particularly relevant research because the intended use is small visual identification/reference thumbnails rather than decorative reproduction, and Washington lies within the Ninth Circuit.

This creates a possible public-use path for some exact official/production thumbnails after per-source review:

- thumbnail-sized and screen-oriented;
- used only to identify/catalog a plate design;
- source/provenance retained;
- no print-ready original distributed;
- no market substitution for source art;
- no implication of government/sponsor endorsement;
- rights-holder removal/permission path available;
- legal review before commercial/public launch at scale.

## 5. Trademark / nominative identification

The Ninth Circuit recognizes nominative fair use where a mark is used truthfully to identify the marked product/service and the use does not suggest sponsorship or endorsement. *Toyota Motor Sales v. Tabari*, 610 F.3d 1171 (9th Cir. 2010), also warns that stylized logos and brand visual trappings can be more than necessary and can increase confusion.

For License Plate Game this means a written team/university/sponsor name may be easier to justify for identification than reproducing its full stylized logo. Logos and other marks remain a separate review item even if copyright treatment is favorable.

## 6. Credential/counterfeit concerns are separate

Accurate plate references should remain screen/reference artifacts and not become practical substitute registration plates. Public reconstructions use fictitious/sample serials, omit usable validation credentials/security features, and are not distributed as manufacturing files. See `REFERENCE_RENDER_SAFETY.md`.

## Working decision tree

1. **Clearly licensed/public-safe official asset?** Use it with attribution/terms.
2. **Can the design be independently reconstructed from factual/legal specs without copying protected expression?** Prefer reconstruction.
3. **Is the protected element separable (logo/seal/sponsor art)?** Reconstruct the base and separately clear, omit, abstract, or evaluate nominative use of the mark.
4. **Is a small exact thumbnail necessary for meaningful identification?** Evaluate source-specific copyright/fair-use/trademark factors; do not automatically publish.
5. **Still uncertain?** Keep the production image internal as verification evidence and publish a safe abstraction until permission/legal review resolves it.

## Product posture

The research database should distinguish **reference acquired**, **accuracy verified**, and **public-use cleared**. None implies the others.
